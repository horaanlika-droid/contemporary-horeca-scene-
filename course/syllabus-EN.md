# CONTEMPORARY HORECA SCENE — SYLLABUS (EN)

**Course proposal for Hotel Institute Montreux · Academic year 2026–27**
Author & course leader: **Egor Tarasenko**, Master in Business Management — Hotel Institute Montreux Alumnus
Format: semester elective · 12 weeks · 36 contact hours (12 × 3 h) · English · BBA & MIB students

> Sources the course is built on: **MICHELIN Guide** (guide.michelin.com / michelin.ru), **The 50 Best / the50.com** (The World's 50 Best Restaurants & Bars), **GreatList** (greatlist.ru), **Diageo World Class** (diageo.com).

---

## 1. Course description

Contemporary HoReCa Scene is a business-perspective tour of the global restaurant and bar industry at this exact moment. It reads the industry through four lenses — **rankings & institutions**, **design & atmosphere**, **neurogastronomy & guest psychology**, and **technology & automation** — and grounds every lens in live case venues: the world's best restaurants and bars.

The course is updated every year: the 2026–27 edition works with the 50 Best lists revealed in Turin (2025), the World's 50 Best Bars 2025 ceremony in Hong Kong, the World Class Global Finals 2025 in Toronto, the 2026 MICHELIN reveals (Tokyo, California, Toronto) and — live, in week 10–11 — the 2026 restaurant ceremony in Lima (4 November 2026).

## 2. Learning outcomes

After the course, students will be able to:

1. Navigate the global ranking ecosystem (MICHELIN, The 50 Best, GreatList, World Class) and use it in marketing, strategy and employer branding.
2. Analyse HoReCa macro-trends and translate them into concrete business decisions.
3. Design a venue concept: interior, ergonomics, atmosphere (light, sound, scent), guest journey (CJM).
4. Apply neurogastronomy and multisensory design to menus, serves and service.
5. Evaluate automation and AI solutions for F&B operations with an ethics filter.
6. Develop and pitch a venue concept benchmarked against the world's best.

## 4. Module detail

### Module 1 — Contemporary HoReCa Trends (weeks 1–3)
Topics: experience economy (chef's tables, counters, storytelling formats); anti-luxury & "cocktail popolari"; sustainability → mindfulness (Green Star → "Mindful Voices"); regional cuisines going global (Californios — first three-star Mexican restaurant, California 2026); gastro-tourism & city branding (50 Best week in Lima, projected ~$2M local spend); new gravity centres (Asia, Middle East, Latin America); hotel F&B renaissance.
Signature cases: Maido (№1, 2025); Tuju (Art of Hospitality 2026); Gerbou, Dubai (Prix Versailles 2025, Emirati fine dining).

### Module 2 — Venue Design & Atmosphere (weeks 4–5)
Topics: concept & narrative; space & ergonomics; light, acoustics, scent; tableware & tactile design; service choreography; design awards as signals (Prix Versailles, Best Bar Design); practice — atmosphere audits of Montreux venues.
Signature cases: GreatList selection criteria (food, service CJM, design, atmosphere "from scent to music"); Himkok (Oslo) — Best Bar Design (sustainable reimagining of a 200-year-old space); Hanu (Dubai) — Seoul aesthetics.

### Module 3 — Neurogastronomy & Guest Experience (weeks 6–7)
Topics: gastrophysics & crossmodal correspondences; sound & taste; colour/shape/weight of tableware; menu psychology & price anchors; memory & emotion; peak–end design; sensory lab.
Signature cases: Charles Spence's research; World Class 2025 multisensory Singleton challenge (record sleeve "That's Amore", cocktail "Between Us"); Tuju's radical personalisation.

### Module 4 — Technology & Automation (weeks 8–9)
Topics: reservations & waitlist; dynamic pricing & revenue management; kitchen/floor automation; data & CRM personalisation; AI in creativity (limits included); delivery & dark kitchens; education tech; ethics & humane workplaces.
Signature cases: Don Julio 1942 AI-artwork serve (World Class 2025); Sesto Senso Academy (Felice Capasso); ranking data & voting systems.

### Module 5 — World's Leading Restaurants & Bars (weeks 10–12)
Topics: MICHELIN anatomy (stars, Bib Gourmand, special awards, inspector method); The 50 Best Academy; bar scene & hotel bars; World Class; regional scenes; GreatList geography; final pitch.
Signature cases: 50 Best 2025 top-10; MICHELIN Tokyo/California/Toronto 2026; World's 50 Best Bars 2025; Felice Capasso (World Class 2025).

## 5. Teaching methodology

- Every session: 45' lecture + 75' case discussion + 60' lab/practice.
- Case method on live venues; sensory labs; field atmosphere audits; guest speakers (chefs, bartenders, restaurateurs, ranking experts); "rankings war room" — live analysis of new lists (incl. Lima, 4 Nov 2026).
- Peer-review culture: concepts defended as in front of investors and inspectors.

## 6. Practical layer — "My Venue" (full brief: `practical-assignments-EN.md`)

- **Assignment 0 (week 1):** each student presents their favourite bar or restaurant (5 min) through the four course lenses; sets the observation bar for the semester.
- **Through-project:** every student builds their own venue (restaurant / bar / hotel F&B / pop-up) all semester — one A4 page or two slides added per week, peer-reviewed in pairs. Milestones: W2 concept & USP → W3 trend memo → W4 design & narrative → W5 atmosphere spec + field audit → W6 signature sensory serves → W7 menu & pricing → W8 operations & tech → W9 AI & ethics → **W10 marketing I (guest shifts & residencies, alcohol-brand partnerships in World Class formats, 90-day launch)** → **W11 marketing II (3-year roadmap to a chosen guide/list)** → W12 final pitch "Open & Operate".

## 9. Fit with HIM

The course extends HIM's philosophy — Swiss hospitality tradition plus contemporary global practice — into the living industry: students learn what world-class venues look like from inside, what rankings reward, and what technology changes. It prepares them for internships and careers in top restaurants, bars and hotel F&B.
