# CONTEMPORARY HORECA SCENE — CASE DATABASE (EN)

One-page profiles for seminar work. Fields: format, city, why it matters, teaching angle.

## Rankings, awards & competitions

**MICHELIN Guide.** Anonymous inspectors; stars (★ outstanding in its category / ★★ worth a detour / ★★★ worth a special journey), Bib Gourmand, Green Star (phased out through 2026 and replaced by the editorial "Mindful Voices" platform), special awards (Service, Sommelier, Young Chef, Exceptional Cocktails, Opening of the Year). There is no application: inspectors visit anonymously and repeatedly. 2026 reveals used in class: Tokyo (Myojaku → three stars; 18 new stars), California (Californios — first Mexican restaurant worldwide with three stars), Toronto (Restaurant Pearl Morissette two stars). History angle: MICHELIN Guide Moscow debuted in 2021 (first stars in Russia/CIS) and was suspended in 2022 — a governance & geopolitics case.

**The 50 Best (the50.com, rebranded as The 50 in 2026).** Global academy of anonymous experts; restaurants' voters name their 8 best experiences; the bars academy numbers 800+ experts, each voting for 8 bars visited in the previous 18 months. The main lists cannot be entered and no venue can apply. Restaurant ceremony 2026 — Lima, 4 Nov (Teatro Municipal); pre-announced 2026 Art of Hospitality Award: Tuju (São Paulo). Bars 2026 revealed in Milan, 7 Oct (Palazzo del Ghiaccio): Bar Leone №1 for the second year, Moebius Milano №2, Alquímico №3, Bar Us №4, Line №5; 26 cities, 14 new entries. Open special awards: Best Bar Design (six published criteria; self-entry), Best Cocktail Menu (design and layout, storytelling, innovation, range, identity; judged by the 29 Academy Chairs), Sustainable Bar, One to Watch, Best New Opening, Highest New Entry, Highest Climber. Teaching angle: voting design, list economics, city-branding effects (~$2M projected spend in Lima), and the difference between a closed ranking and open-call awards.

**Tales of the Cocktail Spirited Awards.** The bar world's best-known jury prize, founded 2007; 20th edition in New Orleans on 23 July 2026. Free public nominations, self-nomination accepted and encouraged, one nomination is enough; 250+ bartenders, owners, writers and industry leaders judge across international, U.S. and global categories. 2026: FURA (Singapore) World's Best Bar and Best International Restaurant Bar; Yayo Nava (Bar Mauro, Mexico City) International Bartender of the Year; Allegory at the Eaton Hotel (Washington, D.C.) World's Best Cocktail Menu; Tlecān (Mexico City) World's Best Spirits Selection. Teaching angle: open call vs academy ranking; how a nomination becomes a shortlist.

**The Pinnacle Guide.** Independent 1-, 2- and 3-pin recognition system for cocktail bars, founded 2022. Self-nomination is the only route: a detailed written application is assessed, followed by spot-check interviews and at least two anonymous visits by trained local reviewers. Modules and weights: front of house 20%, drinks programme 25%, look and feel 20%, staff management 20%, operations 10%, community 5%; environmental practice runs through all modules. Pins last up to two years. 2026: 46 bars added; the 33 bars pinned in 2024 were reappraised — 7 upgraded, 26 retained, none downgraded. Teaching angle: the most audit-like model; rewards systems, staff practice and consistency, not one photogenic evening.

**Europe's 50 Best Bars (now The 50 Best Bars Europe).** Inaugural edition in Amsterdam on 30 June 2026 — the first pan-European bar ranking. 300+ anonymous, gender-balanced voters across 11 sub-regions; each names seven best bar experiences of the previous 18 months (up to five at home plus two abroad); no entry fee and no criteria. Line (Athens) №1, The Bar in Front of the Bar №2, Sips №3, Himkok №4, Bar Nouveau №5. Teaching angle: a regional layer that turns local reputations into international shortlists — and a stepping stone the main list cannot offer.

**Restaurant & Bar Design Awards.** Global design competition with open entry; entrants submit written criteria, at least six specified products and up to ten photographs; judged by a design panel. Teaching angle: design jury logic — intention, materials, plans and photography — versus a hospitality ranking that judges the lived room.

**GreatList (greatlist.ru).** International guide founded 2022; 100+ experts; anonymous, self-paid, repeated visits; criteria: food & chef imagination; service across the full CJM; design & ergonomics; atmosphere from scent to music. Cities: Moscow, St. Petersburg, Yekaterinburg, Kazan, Nizhny Novgorod, Russian Far East; Dubai; Doha; Bangkok; Hong Kong; Shanghai; Singapore & Seoul soon. GreatList Sessions 2025 — guest dinners in Moscow by world chefs. Teaching angle: an alternative, city-expert rating model; how to audit atmosphere.

**Diageo World Class.** 16 seasons; 51 countries in 2025; finals in Toronto; winner Felice Capasso (Norway, Nedre Løkka Cocktailbar, Oslo; founder, Sesto Senso Academy). Challenges: Johnnie Walker Black Label classic reinventions ("Top Notes" after the French 75); Don Julio 1942 serve inspired by an AI artwork; Singleton multisensory serve with a custom "That's Amore" record sleeve. Jury: Eric Van Beek (Handshake Speakeasy), Monica Berg (Tayēr + Elementary), Ago Perrone (Connaught Bar). Mission "drink better, not more"; 450,000+ bartenders trained. Teaching angle: competitions as industry R&D and talent market; AI as creative brief.

**Alchemist — Copenhagen, №5.** Multi-hour holistic experience, 50 impressions. Angle: experience economy at the extreme.

**Gaggan — Bangkok, №6.** Progressive Indian, emoji menu legacy. Angle: reinvention after collapse.

**Restaurant Pearl Morissette — Niagara (Toronto guide), two stars 2026.** Angle: terroir-driven tasting menus outside capitals.

## Bars

*Unless a year is stated otherwise, positions in this section refer to The 50 Best Bars 2025; the 2026 top ten is summarised in the rankings entry above.*

**Bar Leone — Hong Kong, №1 The 50 Best Bars 2025 & 2026.** "Cocktail popolari"; №1 two years after opening; first Asian №1; the mortadella sandwich became part of the story. Angle: simplicity, warmth, hospitality-first design; neighbourhood roots at global scale.

**Line — Athens, №5 The 50 Best Bars 2026; №1 Europe's 50 Best Bars 2026; Ketel One Sustainable Bar Award 2026.** Fermentation-driven, circular production and zero-waste principles shape the menu and the guest experience. Angle: sustainability as a top-five competitive strategy, not a side category.

**FURA — Singapore, World's Best Bar and Best International Restaurant Bar, Spirited Awards 2026.** Amoy Street shophouse opened in 2023 by Christina Rasmussen (former Noma forager) and Sasha Wijidessa; "Journal of Future Food" with invasive species (Jellyfish Martini), insect protein, cell-cultured milk, surplus-produce ferments, an upcycled interior and a dedicated low-/no-alcohol section. Two Pinnacle pins. Angle: a planet-forward concept that wins on flavour and storytelling, not worthiness.

**Panda & Sons — Edinburgh, Siete Misterios Best Cocktail Menu 2026 (Europe and world) for Transcend.** Sub-zero techniques and a menu designed as an artefact that carries the bar's identity. Angle: the menu as the most portable piece of the concept — and the one a jury can read without visiting.

**Handshake Speakeasy — Mexico City, №2 (№1 in 2024).** Eric Van Beek; hidden-door dramaturgy, flawless execution. Angle: speakeasy as theatre.

**Sips / Paradiso — Barcelona, №3/№4 (2025); Sips №3 on Europe's 50 Best Bars 2026, Paradiso №17 and Legend of the List 2026.** Barcelona as cocktail capital; Sips = counterless island and bespoke vessels; Paradiso = hidden-world entrance. Angle: city cluster effects and the difference between a global ranking and a new regional one.

**Tayēr + Elementary — London, №5 (Monica Berg).** Innovation + sustainability leadership. Angle: R&D culture in a bar.

**Connaught Bar — London, №6 (Ago Perrone).** Hotel bar excellence; trolley ritual. Angle: heritage luxury, hotel F&B renaissance.

**Himkok — Oslo.** Best Bar Design (sustainable reimagining of a 200-year-old space); in-house distillery. Angle: design awards, circular production.

**The Cambridge Public House — Paris.** Sustainable Bar award, huge climber. Angle: sustainability as competitive strategy.

**Mimi Kakushi — Dubai (Best Bar in Middle East) / Hero Bar — Nairobi (Best Bar in Africa).** Angle: the widening map of cocktail culture.

## Budget builds & scenography

**Joi Espresso Bar — the course author's own project.** Assembled almost entirely from the street and flea markets: furniture, fixtures, equipment and objects other people had already discarded, adjusted, repaired and re-finished by hand until the room held together as one story. No large investment behind it. Angle: you do not always need enormous budgets to open something with soul — a point of view and patience in sourcing do the work; money buys speed and finish, intention buys soul.

**Pacific — the course author's bar-equipment design and fabrication studio.** Pacific designs stations and consoles around the actual sequence of service: a sintered-stone top, recessed ice well, speed rails, an under-counter glass hanger, mobile castor-mounted furniture, 3D visualisations and workshop-ready technical drawings. Angle: ergonomics is scenography the guest never notices; design the team's movement, reach and tools before the workshop builds the bar.

**Scenography as a budget tool.** Theatrical decorative techniques replace expensive materials: painted flats that read as marble or plaster, forced perspective that deepens a small room, backdrops instead of architecture, scrim and gauze instead of joinery, trompe-l'œil, glazing, patina, distressing, stencil and gold leaf, faux bois / faux marbre, re-upholstery, haze and a single tight beam of light. Angle: the stage builds believable worlds for one evening on almost nothing — a venue can borrow the entire toolbox, provided the trick never announces itself.

**The fairy-tale test.** A bar or restaurant is a sweet fairy tale; any small detail can instantly wake the guest from that dream — a harsh light, a plastic tray, a visible printer, a dirty door handle. Angle: use the test as an audit question for every design, sourcing and service decision.

## Leading industry figures by course block

**Jiro Ono — Sukiyabashi Jiro (Ginza, Tokyo, 3★ MICHELIN).** *Blocks: Module 1 (Rankings & Mastery) & Module 3 (Neurogastronomy & Counter Rhythm).* A 10-seat basement counter in a Ginza subway station with no printed menu and no decor excess, holding three MICHELIN stars for decades. Jiro serves a 20-piece nigiri progression timed to the guest's pace, with *shari* (sushi rice) held at 37°C body temperature and piece size adjusted to each guest. Angle: *shokunin* mastery, peak–end orchestration, and proof that prestige comes from daily perfection of fundamentals rather than real estate.

**Simone Caporale — Sips Drinkery House (Barcelona, №1 World’s 50 Best Bars 2023).** *Blocks: Module 2 (Experience Design & Counterless Hospitality) & Module 6 (Creative Briefs).* Co-founder of Sips with Marc Álvarez, leading it to №1 in The World’s 50 Best Bars 2023, and former creative pillar of London’s Artesian (four consecutive years World №1). Sips dismantled the traditional bar counter: bartenders work at an open central island workstation with 360-degree visibility, using bespoke sculptural vessels (such as cast metal hands) and culinary mixology without five-star stiffness. Angle: spatial democracy, removing barriers between maker and guest, and bespoke tactile vessels as theatrical storytelling.

**Artem Talalay — London Bar (Sochi), Diageo Reserve World Class Russia Winner, Palm Branch Hall of Fame.** *Blocks: Module 3 (Neurogastronomy & Sensory Mixology) & Module 4/5 (Speed Ergonomics).* Winner of World Class Russia (2020–2021) in both *Signature Drink* and *Cocktail Against the Clock*. Defines modern mixology across four coordinates — taste, aroma, enveloping tactile texture and conceptual colouristics — while engineering station ergonomics to deliver high-concept serves at peak bar speed. Angle: translating gastrophysics into reproducible 60-second execution.

**Rémy Savage — A Bar with Shapes for a Name (London), Bar Nouveau (Paris), Abstract (Lyon), Little Red Door, Artesian.** *Blocks: Module 4 (Restaurant & Bar Concepts) & Module 6 (AI & Conceptual Authorship).* World Class Bartender of the Year (2014) who builds bars as complete philosophical and artistic manifestos — Bauhaus primary geometry and functionalism at *Shapes*, Art Nouveau organic lines at *Bar Nouveau*, monochrome distillation at *Abstract*. Angle: a concept is not a moodboard; it is a strict rulebook that dictates glassware, furniture, ice, batching and what does *not* belong in the room.

**Bek Narzi — City Space Bar (Moscow), Pachamama (London), author of The Horeca Code & Seven Hours Before Take-off.** *Blocks: Module 4/5 (Operations & Standards) & Module 5/8 (Entrepreneurship & Talent School).* British-Russian hospitality entrepreneur who put *City Space Bar* into the World's 50 Best Bars, mentored a generation of bar founders (including Igor Zernov), co-created bar innovations such as dehydrated fruit chips with hotel pastry chefs, and codified bar management in *The Horeca Code*. Angle: creative showmanship only survives on top of iron operational standards, unit economics and a disciplined school of people.

**Hiroyasu Kayama — Bar Benfiddich (Shinjuku, Tokyo, №18 World's 50 Best Bars / №9 Asia's 50 Best Bars).** *Blocks: Module 3 (Olfactory Ritual) & Module 5/7 (Food & Beverage Futures — Farm-to-Glass).* In a 16-seat apothecary room (*Ben* = mountain = *yama*, *Fiddich* = deer = *ka* → *Kayama*), Kayama works with zero printed menu, crushing wormwood, fennel, juniper, mint and yuzu grown on his family farm in Chichibu and pouring house-distilled absinthe and amari. When he travels, the bar stays closed. Angle: owning the raw ingredient from soil to glass and refusing industrial scale to protect authenticity.

**Igor Zernov — El Copitas Bar (№8 World's 50 Best Bars) & #FollowTheRabbits (St. Petersburg).** *Blocks: Module 1/4 (Concepts & Community Ritual) & Module 5/8 (Entrepreneurship — Speakeasy to Ecosystem).* Alumnus of Bek Narzi's *City Space* school and co-founder of *#FollowTheRabbits* (*El Copitas*, *Paloma Cantina*, *Tagliatella Caffe*, *Sangre Fresca*, *Bartenders FAQtory*, *Saint-Petersburg Cocktail Week*). Turned a tiny hidden courtyard bar built around one communal table and a welcome taco + copita of mezcal into a top-10 global bar and multi-concept group. Angle: low-capex validation, community warmth, and scaling through distinct concepts and industry education.

**Denis Bobkov — Pub Life Group: Black Swan Pub & Shop, Bambule, Abbey Players, The Bix, Tap & Barrel, Drunken Duck (Moscow).** *Blocks: Module 2 (Experience Design & "Sweet Fairy Tale") & Module 6/9 (Budget Realisation & Salvage Scenography).* Rose from dishwasher, waiter, cook and bartender to co-owner of Pub Life Group, creating Europe's most atmospheric multi-room theatrical pubs and bars. Assembles labyrinthine worlds from European salvage yards, flea markets, antique church doors, Victorian confessionals, vintage brass and stage lighting. Angle: salvage architecture and theatrical scenography keep the "sweet fairy tale" intact far more convincingly than catalogue luxury.

**Boris Zarkov — White Rabbit Family & Krasota Gastro-Theatre (Moscow & Dubai).** *Blocks: Module 4 (Concept Design & Immersive Scenography) & Module 8 (Entrepreneurship & Portfolio Strategy).* Founder and CVO of White Rabbit Family and creator of Krasota immersive gastro-theatre. Krasota unites a 20-seat circular interactive table with 360-degree video projections, spatial acoustics and synchronized tasting courses. Angle: hospitality as total artwork (Gesamtkunstwerk) where digital scenography, storytelling and dining pacing merge.

**René Redzepi — Noma & Noma Projects (Copenhagen / global residencies).** *Blocks: Module 7 (Food & Beverage Futures) & Module 10 (Final Challenge).* Noma’s co-founder helped bring Nordic ingredients, foraging, hyper-seasonality and fermentation into the global restaurant conversation. In 2026, after former employees made public allegations of workplace abuse, Redzepi acknowledged harmful past leadership, apologised and stepped away from Noma. Angle: study the culinary influence alongside questions of worker dignity, leadership and accountability; creative excellence never excuses harm.

**Dave Arnold — Liquid Intelligence & Booker & Dax (New York).** *Blocks: Module 3 (Neurogastronomy and Drink R&D) & Module 7 (Food & Beverage Futures).* Author of *Liquid Intelligence: The Art and Science of the Perfect Cocktail*, food-and-drink educator, innovator and founder of the Museum of Food and Drink. His measurement-first approach tests temperature, dilution, carbonation, sugar-acid balance and clarification. Angle: change one variable, measure, taste and repeat until the technique is both expressive and operationally reliable.

**Alex Kratena — Artesian at The Langham & Tayēr + Elementary (London).** *Blocks: Module 2 (Experience Design) & Module 4 (Bar Concepts).* Former head bartender of Artesian, which reached No. 1 in The World’s 50 Best Bars for four consecutive years with his team; in 2019 he co-founded Tayēr + Elementary with Monica Berg. The venue pairs an approachable neighbourhood bar with a produce-led cocktail R&D counter. Angle: make innovation accessible without flattening the identity of the more experimental offer.
