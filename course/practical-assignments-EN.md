# CONTEMPORARY HORECA SCENE — PRACTICAL ASSIGNMENTS (EN)

The course has one through-line: **each student builds their own venue — a bar or a restaurant — from idea to "open and operate"**. Every lesson hands over one part of that project; the numbered assignments follow the lesson sequence, so Assignment 03 belongs to Lesson 03 and Assignment 12 to the final lesson.

`lessons.txt` in the repository root is the authored source for the lessons and for these briefs; the app publishes the same text. Lessons 01 and 02 open the sequence with a short in-app challenge (the award map and the three-scene atmosphere map) before the numbered assignments begin.

Every brief below ends the same way, as it does in the app: keep the work on **one sketch page** — a working page from a designer's notebook rather than a finished sheet — and gather the twelve pages into one board before the final pitch.

## From Creative Research to Open & Operate

Each assignment develops one part of the student's final hospitality concept. The work should accumulate throughout the course, allowing the final submission to bring together the concept, menu, guest experience, operating model, sourcing strategy, and physical mockup.

## LESSON 03 — THE SENSES AT THE TABLE, VESSEL WEIGHT & FLAVOUR ARCHITECTURE

**Assignment 03: The Sensory Serve**

**Objective**

Develop one signature drink or dish through sensory research, demonstrating how flavour, aroma, texture, colour, and presentation work together.

**Instructions**

1. Define the central idea of one signature serve and the experience it should create.
2. Map its sensory characteristics: taste, aroma, texture, colour, temperature, and mouthfeel.
3. Select two or three possible vessels or serving formats and explain how each changes the perceived experience.
4. Create a simple tasting and presentation prototype. Record observations about balance, aroma, visual impact, and usability.
5. Explain how the serve reflects the intended identity of your future venue.

**Submission**

* One-page sensory concept sheet.
* A sensory map of the signature serve.
* Reference images or photographs of the prototype.
* A short explanation of the vessel choice and its role in the experience.

**Deliverable:** One signature serve with a clear sensory rationale.

## LESSON 04 — CONCEPT MANIFESTOS & DECONSTRUCTING THE WORLD’S 50 BEST MENUS

**Assignment 04: The Menu Manifesto**

**Objective**

Create a conceptual foundation for a menu that expresses a distinctive hospitality identity rather than simply listing products.

**Instructions**

1. Write a short concept manifesto describing the venue's point of view, cultural references, intended audience, and emotional promise.
2. Research three relevant menus or hospitality concepts, including examples that demonstrate different editorial approaches.
3. Analyse their structure, language, visual identity, guest navigation, and relationship to the venue's story.
4. Identify the principles you want to adopt, adapt, or deliberately reject.
5. Develop a preliminary menu architecture: for example, by ingredient, flavour, season, place, narrative, or occasion.
6. Explain how the menu will make the venue recognisable without copying an existing business.

**Submission**

* Concept manifesto.
* Three short menu case-study analyses.
* A proposed menu structure.
* A visual reference board.

**Deliverable:** A clear editorial direction for the venue's Menu Concept.

## LESSON 05 — CONCEPT OBJECTS: THE MENU, THE MERCHANDISE & THE FURNITURE

**Assignment 05: The Object Family**

**Objective**

Translate the venue's concept into a coherent family of physical objects that guests encounter and use.

**Instructions**

1. Select three objects that are central to the guest experience: a menu, a service object, and a furniture or merchandise element.
2. Define the purpose of each object and the moment in the guest journey when it appears.
3. Choose appropriate materials, finishes, dimensions, and visual references.
4. Consider touch, durability, cleaning, storage, repair, and replacement.
5. Estimate a realistic unit cost or sourcing range for each object.
6. Explain how the objects relate visually and conceptually to one another.

**Submission**

* Three object concept sheets.
* Material and reference board.
* Preliminary dimensions and material specifications.
* Estimated sourcing or production costs.

**Deliverable:** A connected object family that expresses the venue's identity and can be developed for real use.

## LESSON 06 — AUTOMATION, ERGONOMICS & THE HORECA CODE

**Assignment 06: The Invisible Operating System**

**Objective**

Design a basic operational workflow that supports excellent hospitality without creating unnecessary complexity for the team.

**Instructions**

1. Map the preparation and service sequence for one signature menu item.
2. Identify repetitive tasks, precision-critical tasks, and moments that require human judgement.
3. Mark where tools, equipment, software, or automation could improve consistency or reduce unnecessary work.
4. Sketch the relevant workstation and indicate the movement of people, ingredients, tools, and finished orders.
5. Identify likely bottlenecks, safety concerns, and ergonomic problems.
6. Explain how the proposed workflow protects time for guest interaction.

**Submission**

* A workflow diagram.
* A simple workstation layout.
* An automation-versus-human-attention matrix.
* A short list of operational risks and proposed improvements.

**Deliverable:** A practical service workflow that balances efficiency, craft, ergonomics, and human contact.

## LESSON 07 — AI AS A CREATIVE AND OPERATIONAL TOOL

**Assignment 07: The Human–AI Development Brief**

**Objective**

Use AI to support concept development while retaining human judgement, authorship, and responsibility.

**Instructions**

1. Select one real task within your hospitality concept, such as menu research, naming, flavour exploration, demand forecasting, or preparation planning.
2. Write a clear brief describing the task, the context, the constraints, and the desired result.
3. Use an AI tool to generate or analyse possible approaches.
4. Document the most useful results, the inaccuracies or weaknesses you identified, and the changes you made.
5. Distinguish between AI-generated suggestions, independently verified information, and your own creative decisions.
6. Write a short AI-use policy covering privacy, factual verification, creative ownership, and appropriate human oversight.

**Submission**

* Original brief and selected AI outputs.
* A short decision log showing what was retained, revised, or rejected.
* Final human-edited result.
* A concise AI ethics charter.

**Deliverable:** A documented example of responsible AI-assisted work relevant to the venue.

## LESSON 08 — THE NEXT TABLE & HIROYASU KAYAMA’S FARM-TO-GLASS APOTHECARY

**Assignment 08: From Origin to Memory**

**Objective**

Develop a signature serve or menu element around an ingredient's origin, seasonality, sensory character, and potential to create a memorable guest experience.

**Instructions**

1. Choose one principal ingredient with a clear connection to a place, season, producer, or personal story.
2. Research its origin, availability, flavour profile, and relevant sourcing considerations.
3. Explore at least two preparation methods, such as infusion, fermentation, clarification, dehydration, or another suitable technique.
4. Compare the results in terms of flavour, texture, aroma, consistency, resource use, and practical feasibility.
5. Develop a preliminary signature serve or menu element around the most promising approach.
6. Explain how the ingredient's story will be communicated honestly to guests.

Use appropriate food-safety practices and do not attempt specialist techniques without suitable equipment, training, and controls.

**Submission**

* Ingredient origin and research sheet.
* Preparation and experimentation notes.
* Sensory comparison.
* Signature serve or menu-element concept.
* Short sourcing and seasonality rationale.

**Deliverable:** One ingredient-led concept connecting origin, technique, flavour, and guest memory.

## LESSON 09 — FROM IDEA TO OPERATING MODEL & TALENT ECOSYSTEM

**Assignment 09: The First Operating Model**

**Objective**

Test whether the proposed venue has a credible commercial structure and a realistic plan for developing its team.

**Instructions**

1. Define the venue format, capacity, opening schedule, target audience, and service periods.
2. Estimate the average check, expected guest volume, and principal revenue streams.
3. Identify the major cost categories, including ingredients, labour, rent, utilities, equipment, and other operating expenses.
4. State your assumptions clearly and estimate the sales level required to cover the principal costs.
5. Identify three to five operating indicators that would help you assess performance.
6. Create a preliminary team structure, identifying key responsibilities and essential training needs.
7. Outline the priorities for the first 90 days, from preparation and staff training to opening and review.

**Submission**

* One-page operating model.
* Preliminary revenue and cost assumptions.
* Basic break-even estimate.
* Team and responsibility chart.
* 90-day launch outline.

**Deliverable:** A first-pass business and talent plan that connects creative ambition to operating reality.

## LESSON 10 — SOUL BEFORE BUDGET: JOI ESPRESSO BAR & DENIS BOBKOV’S SALVAGE WORLDS

**Assignment 10: The Salvage & Sourcing Plan**

**Objective**

Develop a material and object-sourcing strategy that gives the venue character while remaining financially and operationally credible.

**Instructions**

1. Define the atmosphere and material language of the venue using a concise visual reference board.
2. Identify three anchor objects that could establish the character of the space.
3. Add supporting objects and smaller details that reinforce the same visual world.
4. Research potential sourcing routes, including second-hand markets, salvage suppliers, existing stock, repair, reuse, and new production where appropriate.
5. Estimate acquisition, transport, restoration, cleaning, and maintenance costs.
6. Identify which objects are suitable for direct reuse and which may need adaptation or replacement for hygiene, durability, accessibility, or safety.
7. Compare the estimated salvage-led approach with a reasonable alternative sourcing scenario.

**Submission**

* Salvage and material moodboard.
* Annotated list of proposed objects.
* Preliminary sourcing and budget table.
* Notes on restoration, maintenance, safety, and operational suitability.

**Deliverable:** A credible sourcing plan showing how the venue's character can be realised within a defined budget.

## LESSON 11 — THEATRICAL TECHNIQUES & THE LIVE MOCKUP FROM FOUND OBJECTS

**Assignment 11: The Live Found-Object Mockup**

**Objective**

Translate the venue's visual identity into a physical scene using found objects, a menu prototype, and considered lighting.

**Instructions**

1. Select a small area of the intended venue to represent, such as a table, counter, service station, or intimate corner.
2. Assemble real objects that support the concept: tableware, glassware, candles or suitable lighting alternatives, textiles, wood, and other materials.
3. Include a physical prototype of the Menu Concept.
4. Compose the objects so that their materials, shapes, and relationships communicate one clear story.
5. Test the scene under lighting appropriate to the intended guest experience.
6. Photograph the mockup from the guest's seated perspective, including a wider view and close-up details.
7. Review the result for visual coherence and practical concerns, such as readability, stability, cleaning, and safe use.

Use safe candle practices and keep flames away from combustible materials.

**Submission**

* Photographs of the live mockup from at least three viewpoints.
* A short explanation of the concept and object choices.
* A list of the principal materials and proposed sourcing routes.
* A brief reflection on what the mockup revealed and what would need to change before implementation.

**Deliverable:** A physical, photographed demonstration of the venue's atmosphere and material identity.

## LESSON 12 — DESIGN THE HOSPITALITY CONCEPT OF TOMORROW: OPEN & OPERATE

**Assignment 12: The Final Open & Operate Pitch**

**Objective**

Bring the course's creative, spatial, sensory, operational, and commercial work together into one coherent hospitality concept.

**Instructions**

Prepare a final presentation that explains the venue as both a guest experience and an operating business.

Include:

1. **Concept and positioning:** venue name, target guest, occasion, and one-sentence USP.
2. **Experience narrative:** the intended atmosphere, guest journey, and defining service moments.
3. **Menu Concept:** editorial direction, physical format, and representative signature offers.
4. **Sensory design:** the role of flavour, aroma, texture, colour, vessel choice, and presentation.
5. **Space and objects:** material palette, furniture, lighting, and key designed objects.
6. **Live mockup:** photographs of the found-object scene and its relationship to the concept.
7. **Operating model:** capacity, average check assumptions, revenue streams, major costs, and performance indicators.
8. **Service and technology:** workflow, ergonomics, automation, and the role of human attention.
9. **Sourcing and budget:** salvage strategy, procurement assumptions, and estimated realisation costs.
10. **People and responsibility:** staffing principles, training, safe working conditions, sourcing responsibility, and relevant operational risks.
11. **Reference principles:** identify one or two practitioners whose ideas influenced your decisions and explain how you adapted those principles.
12. **Implementation priorities:** identify the major steps required to move from concept to opening.

Make the relationships between the sections explicit. The menu should support the USP; the service model should support the menu; the budget should support the design; and the mockup should demonstrate the atmosphere promised by the presentation.

Clearly label estimates and assumptions. Do not present unverified projections as established results.

**Submission**

* The twelve sketch pages gathered into one board — the venue as one spread, and the pitch is read from it.
* Complete Open & Operate pitch deck.
* Physical or photographed Menu Concept.
* Sourcing and budget plan.
* Photographs of the live found-object mockup.
* Supporting operating-model and service-workflow materials.

**Deliverable:** A complete hospitality concept and mockup that communicates a distinctive guest experience, a coherent design language, and a credible plan for responsible operation.

## HOW THE ASSIGNMENTS CONNECT

The assignments are designed to build on one another:

* **Lessons 03–04:** establish the sensory signature and conceptual voice.
* **Lessons 05–06:** translate the identity into objects and workable systems.
* **Lesson 07:** integrate AI thoughtfully into research and development.
* **Lesson 08:** develop ingredient-led creativity and a credible origin story.
* **Lesson 09:** test the operating model and team structure.
* **Lesson 10:** establish the sourcing strategy and budget.
* **Lesson 11:** make the concept tangible through a live mockup.
* **Lesson 12:** bring the accumulated work together in the final Open & Operate proposition.

The final project should not feel like a collection of separate exercises. It should demonstrate how each decision contributes to the same hospitality experience — from the first impression to the final operating detail.
