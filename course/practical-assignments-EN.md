# CONTEMPORARY HORECA SCENE — PRACTICAL ASSIGNMENTS (EN)

The course has one through-line: **each student builds their own venue — a bar or a restaurant — from idea to "open and operate"**. Weekly milestones convert every module into a decision about the student's own concept. Final deliverable: a complete concept deck pitched on week 12.

Grading weights (see syllabus):
- Assignment 0 + participation — 10%
- Field Notes (weekly one-page reviews) — 15%
- "My Venue" weekly milestones — 35%
- Final pitch "Open & Operate" — 40%

---

## Assignment 0 — Week 1 · "My favourite bar or restaurant" (5-min talk)

**Goal:** warm-up, diagnostics, and a shared library of real references for the whole semester.
**Format:** 5 minutes, 3 slides max (or no slides).
**Content:**
1. The venue: city, format, one photo.
2. Why I love it — through the course's four lenses:
   - what trend it rides (experience, anti-luxury, regional identity…);
   - design & atmosphere (light, sound, scent, space);
   - what it does to your senses and memory (neurogastronomy in plain words);
   - what it would need to enter a ranking (MICHELIN / The 50 Best / GreatList / a bar award) — or why it's already there.
3. One thing you would change as its owner.
**Rubric:** specificity (40%) · use of the four lenses (40%) · honesty of observation (20%).
**Rule:** no famous venues "because it's famous" — you must have visited it yourself.

## The through-line project "My Venue"

Each student chooses their own concept in week 2 (restaurant, bar, hotel F&B, pop-up). Every week adds one page/section to the concept. Format: one A4 page or 2 slides per milestone; peer review in pairs every week.

### W2 · Concept & USP
- City & district, audience, format, price point.
- **USP in one sentence**: what only this venue gives.
- Three benchmarks from the course map (50 Best / MICHELIN / GreatList venues) and what exactly is borrowed vs. invented.
*In-class:* 60-second USP elevator pitch, voted by the group.

### W3 · Trend memo
- Which 2–3 macro-trends the concept rides; which it deliberately ignores.
- One risk per trend (fad fatigue, cost, staffing).
*Deliverable:* trend radar one-pager.

### W4 · Design & narrative
- The story the venue tells; interior "three scenes" (entrance, main hall, the "instagrammable" corner).
- Zoning sketch + ergonomics: guest flow, service flow, noise map.
*Reference:* GreatList criteria; Prix Versailles; Himkok.

### W5 · Atmosphere spec
- Light (3 evening states), sound (3 playlists with BPM logic), scent, tableware.
- Service choreography: greeting ritual, peak moment, farewell.
- **Field task:** audit one real venue in Montreux with the GreatList-style scorecard; one actionable recommendation.

### W6 · Neurogastronomy I — signature experiences
- Three signature dishes/serves built on sensory effects (sound pairing, weight/texture, contrast, surprise).
- For each: intended perception and the "Proust moment" it targets.
*Lab:* test one of them in the sensory lab, record peer feedback.

### W7 · Menu & pricing
- Menu architecture: categories, anchors, decoys; naming rules.
- Draft menu with price logic (no currency-sign column, sequencing).
- One A/B test designed for launch month.

### W8 · Operations & technology
- Service model and staffing sketch; table turns / cover plan.
- Tech stack choices: reservations & waitlist, CRM, KDS/inventory — and what stays human.
*Exercise:* human/machine matrix for your own evening timeline.

### W9 · AI & ethics page
- Where AI helps (briefs, forecasts, content) and where it is banned in your concept.
- One labour-practice rule you commit to (humane scheduling, training budget).

### W10 · Marketing I — partnerships & guest formats
Design the marketing engine:
- **Guest shifts & residencies:** which world bartenders/chefs to invite and why (GreatList Sessions model); frequency and economics.
- **Alcohol-brand partnerships:** format like Diageo World Class — brand residencies, education nights, signature series with Johnnie Walker/Tanqueray/Don Julio-type partners; what the venue gets vs. gives.
- Non-alcohol & local collabs; coffee/tea/water partnerships.
- Launch campaign: 90-day plan (teaser, opening, first reviews).

### W11 · Marketing II — roadmap to recognition
- Target list(s): MICHELIN / The 50 Best / GreatList / bar awards — pick one primary.
- 3-year plan: what to change each year to deserve it; press & academy-relations ethics.
- Loyalty & community: regulars programme, "friends of the house".

### W12 · Final pitch — "Open & Operate"
Full deck (10–12 slides / 10 minutes + Q&A):
1. USP & story; 2. trends; 3. design & atmosphere board; 4. menu & beverage (neurogastronomy); 5. operations & tech; 6. marketing engine (guest shifts, brand partnerships, launch); 7. roadmap to recognition; 8. unit-economics sketch (average check, covers, rent logic, payback horizon); 9. team & values; 10. the one slide that makes the panel remember you.
**Panel:** lecturer + invited industry professionals. Verdicts: "ready to open", "open after fixes", "reconcept".

## Field Notes (weekly, all semester)
One page, every Friday: a venue visit, a ranking change, or an industry article — three observations + one implication for "My Venue".

## Rubric for weekly milestones (shared)

- Use of course tools & sources — 30%
- Craft & presentation — 20%
- Peer-review quality (you review a partner's page) — 10%
